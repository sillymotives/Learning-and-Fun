# Section 4 candidate recommendation: a measured November sales promotion

**Status:** Agreed proposal for the final report, *not* evidence of proven campaign effectiveness and not yet a completed Section 4.

## Observation from the supplied Q4 2020 dataset

| Month | Recorded sales values | Days with records |
|---|---:|---:|
| October | 114,290,000 | 30 |
| November | 90,682,500 | 30 |
| December | 135,330,000 | 30 |

November's recorded sales were approximately **20.7% below October's**, and December was higher still. Every month has 30 recorded days, but 31 October and 31 December are absent. The source does not state its currency. A single quarter does not establish that lower November sales are a recurring pattern or that the company was not already running promotions.

## Recommendation for the Head of Sales & Marketing

**Pilot a targeted November sales campaign, potentially aligned with Black Friday, before committing to a national rollout.** Black Friday in 2020 fell on **27 November**. The purpose is to test whether a carefully timed offer can increase **incremental gross profit**, especially in weaker state–demographic combinations, without sacrificing the stronger December period.

Possible pilot areas for further research:
- **Western Australia:** consider testing Women's product assortments or targeted creative; Men currently contribute the highest recorded sales among groups there.
- **Northern Territory:** investigate Seniors' preferences and test a relevant offer; Men lead this territory's recorded sales.

These choices are **hypotheses for experiments**, not evidence that Women in WA or Seniors in NT respond better to discounts. All four groups contribute materially in each state. The dataset does **not** justify dropping any demographic group or excluding customers.

## Proposed pilot design

1. Gather missing commercial information: product margin, cost of goods, available stock, store count, returns, footfall, existing promotions, customer transactions, and campaign costs.
2. Where practical, compare matched stores or audiences randomly assigned to the promotion versus no promotion during comparable November dates; record a pre-test baseline.
3. Test a limited, transparent promotion in selected combinations of store, product and daypart rather than discounting the entire range.
4. Measure **incremental contribution profit after discount and campaign costs**, additional units, sales, stockouts, return rates and customer response.
5. Check December and combined November–December performance to detect demand shifted from December into November, not truly created.
6. Expand the test only if measured incremental profit and customer outcomes justify the investment.

**Discount arithmetic caution:** a 15% price cut combined with 10% more units would yield `0.85 × 1.10 = 0.935` of the original sales revenue, or **6.5% less revenue**, even before considering variable costs. More sales volume alone does not prove a profitable campaign.

## Limits and evidence distinction

**Supported by the supplied dataset:** November has the lowest recorded monthly sales of Q4 2020; WA and NT are among the lower-selling regions; customer-group mix differs slightly by state.

**Suggested but unverified:** a Black Friday promotional pilot will outperform existing activity; particular demographics respond more favorably; any previous absence of a promotion; and the profitability of discounts.

**Not observable in the dataset:** individual customer spending, basket size, unit price changes, margin, promotional attribution, stock availability, store count, campaign expense, footfall or year-on-year November seasonality.

This recommendation will become the lead candidate in Section 4's final marketing recommendations, subject to transparent limitations and a report-level synthesis.
