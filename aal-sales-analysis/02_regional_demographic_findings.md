# AAL: state-by-demographic analysis (Q4 2020)

This report supports Section 2c of `notebooks/AAL_Sales_Analysis.ipynb`. Figures are recorded sales values and **not confirmed currency amounts or profit**.

## Lower-selling states and territories

| State/territory | Highest group | Highest sales | Lowest group | Lowest sales | Lowest group share |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| WA | Men | 5,752,500 | Women | 5,262,500 | 23.76% |
| NT | Men | 5,762,500 | Seniors | 5,465,000 | 24.20% |
| TAS | Kids | 5,775,000 | Women | 5,577,500 | 24.51% |
| QLD | Kids | 8,510,000 | Seniors | 8,190,000 | 24.51% |

All groups contribute roughly one-quarter of sales in each state. The largest difference between a state's strongest and lowest group is approximately **2.21 percentage points (WA)**. The figures do not justify dropping any demographic group on revenue grounds.

## Preliminary tests worth investigating

- **WA:** test Women's assortment or messaging while keeping popular Men's stock available.
- **NT:** research Seniors' preferences and test an offer, while supporting the stronger Men's category.
- **TAS:** test a targeted Women's range and consider family-oriented promotions.
- **QLD:** test ways to serve Seniors better without withdrawing the strong Kids range.

This is a starting point, not evidence that the suggested activities cause revenue growth. Before reducing a whole range, compare store counts, footfall, market size, inventory, stock turn, gross margin, customer experience and incremental profit in controlled trials.

The **higher/lower spend question** is treated separately in [02_spending_intensity_findings.md](02_spending_intensity_findings.md). Sales rows are aggregates, not customer transactions, so they cannot identify high-spending people.

**Data caveats:** 31 October and 31 December 2020 are unrecorded; holiday closures remain unverified. The dataset has no ACT observations and does not specify currency.
